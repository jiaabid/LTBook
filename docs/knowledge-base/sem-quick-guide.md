# Scanning Electron Microscope: Quick Operating Guide

> **Fictional sample content.** This guide was written for the ToolBook demo. It is not a real instrument manual. Do not use it to operate real equipment.

## Before you start

You must have completed the SEM induction with lab staff before your first booking. Check in on ToolBook when you arrive, so the next user can see the instrument is in use.

Wear nitrile gloves when handling samples and stubs. Never touch the inside of the chamber or the sample holder with bare hands, because skin oils outgas and degrade the vacuum.

## Sample preparation

Samples must be dry, clean and stable under vacuum. Mount the sample on an aluminium stub using conductive carbon tape. Non-conductive samples (polymers, glass, biological material) must be sputter-coated with 5–10 nm of gold or carbon to prevent charging.

The maximum sample height is 25 mm and the maximum diameter is 50 mm. Measure the height with the height gauge before loading. A sample that is too tall can hit the pole piece and damage the detector.

## Venting and loading the chamber

1. Make sure the electron beam is switched off (the "Beam On" indicator in the software is grey).
2. Click **Vent** in the vacuum panel. Venting takes about 3 minutes.
3. When the status shows "Vented", open the chamber door slowly.
4. Insert the stub into the holder and tighten the set screw gently.
5. Close the door and click **Pump**. Wait until the vacuum status shows "Ready" (typically 4–6 minutes) before switching on the beam.

## Imaging

Start with an accelerating voltage of 5 kV and a working distance of 10 mm. Use the secondary electron (SE) detector for surface topography and the backscattered electron (BSE) detector for compositional contrast.

To focus, first find a feature at low magnification (around 500x), then increase magnification step by step and refocus at each step. Correct astigmatism at high magnification using the stigmator X and Y controls until the image sharpens evenly in all directions.

If the image drifts or shows bright streaks, the sample is probably charging. Lower the accelerating voltage to 2–3 kV, reduce the beam current, or coat the sample.

## Shutting down

1. Switch off the beam and wait 30 seconds.
2. Vent the chamber, remove your sample, close the door and pump down again. Always leave the chamber under vacuum.
3. Log out of the microscope software.
4. Check out on ToolBook so the actual end time is recorded.

## Troubleshooting

**The chamber does not pump down.** Check that the door is fully closed and that the O-ring is clean. If the vacuum is not "Ready" after 15 minutes, vent, inspect the O-ring and try again. If it still fails, contact lab staff and do not leave the pump running unattended.

**The image is completely black.** Check that the beam is on, the correct detector is selected and the brightness is not at zero. Confirm the working distance matches the stage position.
